01

Overview

What the AJS web platform is, who works in it, and how it relates to the mobile app.

AJS is a field-service and compliance management platform for UK social housing and property maintenance.

It carries one continuous record from the moment a client and property are set up, through the contract that governs the work, the job that is raised, the engineer visit that delivers it, the certificate that evidences it, and the batch and invoice that get it paid. This handbook covers the web platform — the office and back-office half. The engineer’s side is documented separately in the AJS Mobile Feature Documentation.

The two halves of one system

This handbook — the web platform

Setting up clients, properties, contracts and rates. Raising and scheduling work. Reviewing and approving what the field returns. Costing, batching and invoicing it. Reporting on all of it.

The mobile handbook — the field

The engineer’s day: receiving tasks, travelling, attending, recording outcomes, capturing photographs, assets, signatures and certificates — online or offline.

Both halves read and write the same records. A status an engineer sets on a doorstep is the status the office sees, and the evidence they capture is what makes the job billable.

How access is controlled.

Each role is granted rights per module at eight levels — view, add, edit, delete, approve, import, export and client scope. Menu items a role cannot view are removed before the page is drawn, and every request is re-checked on the server. The specific rights held by each role are environment configuration and are set per deployment, so they are not reproduced here.

Residents

Residents are not users and have no account. They receive appointment offers by email or text, confirm or decline through a secure single-purpose link, and can track the engineer on the day. These pages are deliberately public.

Operating model

Office

Set up & raise

Clients, properties, contracts and rates are configured once. Work is then raised against them — manually, automatically from a planned programme, or received from a client’s own system.

Field

Deliver & evidence

Engineers receive tasks on the mobile app, attend, record the outcome and capture the photographs, readings, assets, signatures and certificates that prove the work was done.

Approve & bill

Returned work is reviewed and approved, costed against the contract rates, grouped into batches and converted into invoices or applications for payment.

Navigating the Platform

How the application is laid out, and how it differs depending on who signs in.

Screen layout

Left sidebar

The main menu. Groups expand to reveal their items. Entries the role cannot access are removed entirely rather than greyed out.

Top bar

Global search, notification bell with unread count, and the user menu for profile and sign-out.

Breadcrumb

Shows where the current screen sits, and provides the route back up to the parent record.

Record tabs

Every major record opens into a tabbed workspace rather than a single long form. Tabs can themselves be permission-gated.

Data grids

Lists page, filter and sort on the server, so a screen showing 25 rows fetches 25 rows regardless of how many exist behind the filter.

Selecting a task opens a detail card beside the grid instead of navigating away, so the user keeps their place in the list.

What differs by role

Administrators and managers

See the fullest menu, including System Admin and the commercial areas. Managers are an oversight role — typically able to review and approve delivered work rather than create it.

Schedulers

Focused on Scheduling, Jobs and the diary. The surrounding record areas are generally visible for context rather than for editing.

Client users

See only their own organisation’s data. Typically read-only with export, so they can take their own reporting away. Client Admin Users have wider rights within their own organisation.

Engineers and RLOs

Do not use the web platform. Their experience is the mobile app, documented in the AJS Mobile Feature Documentation.

Features

Fifty-nine features, each described as what it does, what it needs, and what happens when it cannot complete.

59 features

Client & Property

FEA-01

Client Records

The master record for each customer organisation that work is delivered for.

Administrators
How it works
  • Clients are created individually or loaded in bulk from a spreadsheet.
  • Each client opens into an eleven-tab workspace covering contracts, properties, rates, finance, messaging and integration.
  • The list pages and filters on the server, and can be exported.
  • Everything else in the platform — property, contract, job, certificate, invoice — belongs to exactly one client.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Clients module.
  • Tabs for Contracts, Properties, Rates and Interface are gated separately.
  • A client cannot be removed from the system — deletion marks the record and retains its history.
If something goes wrong
  • Missing required details are reported against the field before the record saves.
  • A bulk upload showing validation errors is not committed — the preview reports which rows failed and why.
Related: FEA-02, FEA-03, FEA-04, FEA-09  ·  Workflow: WF-01
FEA-02

Client & AJS Contacts

The people on both sides of the relationship, and who to reach for what.

Administrators
How it works
  • Two contact networks are held per client — the client’s own people, and the AJS staff who manage that account.
  • Contacts can be attached to a contract as named personnel.
  • Contact templates define reusable roles so each new client is set up consistently.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Clients module.
  • Contacts attached to a live contract remain referenced there if removed from the client.
If something goes wrong
  • Invalid email or telephone formats are rejected at entry, since these are used for real messaging.
Related: FEA-01, FEA-10
FEA-03

Client Finance & Uplifts

The commercial terms that govern how this client’s work is priced and billed.

Administrators
How it works
  • Selling and billing arrangements, tax treatment and discounts are held per client.
  • Rate uplifts record agreed future increases, with reminders issued before they take effect.
  • Contract-level finance can override the client default where an agreement differs.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Clients module.
  • Uplift reminders are issued by a daily scheduled process, not on demand.
If something goes wrong
  • Work already raised keeps the terms it was raised under — changing finance settings does not retrospectively re-price existing jobs.
Related: FEA-16, FEA-36  ·  Rules: BR-031, BR-032
FEA-04

Client Code Mapping

Translates the client’s own reference codes to AJS codes so exchanged data lines up.

Administrators
How it works
  • Each client can use different codes for the same thing — a job type, a priority, a work category.
  • Mapping pairs the client’s value with the AJS equivalent in both directions.
  • Used by the integration when work orders arrive and when updates are returned.
Conditions
  • Only relevant where the client has an active integration.
  • Requires rights on the Clients module.
If something goes wrong
  • An unmapped code arriving from a client system causes that record to be held in the error folder rather than imported incorrectly.
Related: FEA-48, FEA-49  ·  Workflow: WF-04
FEA-05

Property Records

Every address in a client’s portfolio — the largest data set in the platform.

All office users
How it works
  • Properties are created individually, loaded in bulk from a spreadsheet, or matched by UPRN from a client reference file.
  • Each property opens into six tabs: Property, Assets, PPM Plans, Open Jobs, Job History and Certificates.
  • A quick-edit option allows changes directly from the list without opening the record.
  • Resident contact details are held and maintained against the address.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Properties module.
  • Every property belongs to exactly one client.
  • The list always pages and filters on the server; the portfolio is never loaded into the browser at once.
If something goes wrong
  • A UPRN that does not match an existing record is reported rather than silently creating a duplicate address.
Related: FEA-06, FEA-07, FEA-08, FEA-50  ·  Rules: BR-020
FEA-06

Property Assets

The equipment at an address — boiler, alarm, lift — and its testing record.

All office users
How it works
  • Assets are held against the property, with sub-assets where equipment has components.
  • Asset types carry their own custom fields, defined by an administrator, so different equipment captures different data.
  • Testing records and photographs accumulate against the asset over its life.
  • Engineers can add a newly-found asset, or link and unlink existing equipment, from site.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Assets module.
  • Asset type fields must be configured before that data can be captured.
If something goes wrong
  • An asset cannot be removed while live work references it.
Related: FEA-05, FEA-24, FEA-33
FEA-07

Property Warnings

Safety and access notes surfaced to the engineer before they attend.

All office users
How it works
  • Warning messages are recorded against an address — for example aggressive dog, vulnerable resident, or asbestos present.
  • They travel with any task at that property and are shown to the engineer on the mobile app.
  • Warnings persist across jobs and years, independent of who is attending.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Properties module to add or clear a warning.
If something goes wrong
  • Clearing a warning is an explicit action and is recorded — warnings do not expire on their own.
Related: FEA-05, FEA-24  ·  Rules: BR-021
FEA-08

Property Compliance History

Every job and certificate ever recorded at an address, in one place.

All office usersClient users
How it works
  • Open Jobs shows what is live at the address right now; Job History shows everything ever done and by whom.
  • Certificates lists the compliance evidence trail, with each document retrievable.
  • PPM Plans shows what recurring work is due and when.
Conditions
  • Client users see only their own organisation’s properties.
  • History is retained even for jobs later cancelled, so the record is complete.
If something goes wrong
  • A property with no history shows an explicit empty state rather than an error.

Contracts & Rates

FEA-09

Contract Records

The agreement that governs what work may be done, at what price, within what timescales.

Administrators
How it works
  • Each contract opens into a thirteen-tab workspace, from Dashboard and Overview through to SOR Variations.
  • Reference numbers, dates, categories, and job and rate prefixes are set on Overview.
  • A contract can be activated or deactivated, closing it to new work without removing its history.
  • Its own dashboard shows delivery position, volumes and outstanding work.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Contracts module.
  • A job cannot be priced or targeted without a contract.
  • Documents can be attached to the contract record.
If something goes wrong
  • Deactivating a contract prevents new work being raised against it but leaves existing jobs to run to completion.
Related: FEA-10 – FEA-13, FEA-17  ·  Rules: BR-030, BR-033
FEA-10

Contract Personnel

Who is attached to the contract, on both the AJS and the client side.

Administrators
How it works
  • Four groups are held: engineers, office staff, AJS contacts and client contacts.
  • Attached engineers form the pool the scheduler allocates from.
  • Named contacts drive who is copied on contract correspondence and reports.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Contracts module.
  • Engineers must exist as users with mobile access before they can be attached.
If something goes wrong
  • Removing an engineer does not unassign work already allocated to them — that must be reassigned explicitly.
Related: FEA-09, FEA-22, FEA-26
FEA-11

Workstreams & Services

The categories of work permitted under a contract, and the services within each.

Administrators
How it works
  • Workstreams group the kinds of work a contract covers; services sit beneath them.
  • Services carry their own expected durations, skills and certificate requirements.
  • A job must fall within a permitted workstream for the contract it is raised under.
  • Services can be added to or removed from a contract, and individually activated or deactivated.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Contracts module.
  • Workstreams and services are defined once in Setup and then attached per contract.
If something goes wrong
  • Removing a service from a contract does not affect jobs already raised using it.
Related: FEA-09, FEA-17, FEA-56
FEA-12

PPM Master Plan

The planned preventive maintenance programme — recurring compliance work raised automatically.

Administrators
How it works
  • The master plan defines which compliance work is due on which properties, and how often.
  • A scheduled process runs every morning at 06:00, checks what is due, and raises the jobs.
  • Plans can also be attached at property level for address-specific requirements.
  • The master plan list shows what has been generated and what remains upcoming.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Contracts module.
  • The contract must be active for planned work to be generated against it.
  • Generation is once daily — it is not immediate on saving a plan.
If something goes wrong
  • If the overnight run does not complete, the work is picked up on the next run rather than skipped.

FEA-13

Contract Notification Settings

Which job events send a message to the resident, and by which channel.

Administrators
How it works
  • Each notifiable event is switched on or off independently for the contract.
  • For each enabled event, Email, SMS or both can be selected.
  • This is step one of a two-step setup — the wording itself is written at client level.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Contracts module.
  • At least one channel must be selected for an enabled event, or nothing will be sent.
If something goes wrong
  • An event enabled with no channel selected is a valid but silent state — no message is sent.
  • Where the client has written no custom wording, the system default template is used rather than sending nothing.

FEA-14

Schedule of Rates

The priced catalogue of work items that makes any job costable.

Administrators
How it works
  • Rate items describe a unit of work and carry a cost and a sell value.
  • Items are organised into groups and attached to contracts, so different clients can pay different prices for the same work.
  • Rate items can require a certificate, linking pricing to compliance.
  • The catalogue runs to roughly 14,700 items.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the SORs module.
  • A rate must be attached to the contract before it can be used on that contract’s jobs.
If something goes wrong
  • Changing a rate does not re-price work already raised — existing jobs keep the value they were priced at.
Related: FEA-15, FEA-19, FEA-36  ·  Rules: BR-040
FEA-15

Rate Variations

Contract rates adjusted for particular units or addresses.

Administrators
How it works
  • A variation overrides the standard contract rate for defined units or specific properties.
  • Variations are listed and maintained per contract, and can be created in bulk.
  • Where a variation applies, it takes precedence when the job is priced.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the SORs module.
  • The base rate must already be attached to the contract.
If something goes wrong
  • Where no variation matches, the standard contract rate applies — pricing never fails through a missing variation.
Related: FEA-14, FEA-19
FEA-16

Rate Uplifts

Agreed future price increases, applied on schedule with advance reminders.

Administrators
How it works
  • An uplift records the agreed increase and the date it takes effect, at client or contract level.
  • A daily scheduled process issues reminders ahead of the effective date.
  • A separate daily process applies uplifts when they fall due.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Clients or Contracts module.
  • Reminders and application are both scheduled, not immediate.
If something goes wrong
  • Work already priced is unaffected — an uplift applies to work raised after its effective date.
Related: FEA-03, FEA-14, FEA-58  ·  Rules: BR-041

Jobs & Tasks

FEA-17

Job Creation

Raising a unit of work against a property under a contract.

All office users
How it works
  • Jobs arrive three ways: raised by the office, generated by the planned programme, or received from a client system.
  • The job records client, property, contract, service, priority and client work order reference.
  • The target date is derived from the contract’s service terms rather than typed in.
  • An existing job can be copied to save re-keying similar work.
  • Jobs can be flagged as emergency, and carry preferred dates and times where the resident has expressed one.
Conditions
  • Requires Add rights on the Jobs module.
  • The contract must be active and must permit the workstream the job falls under.
  • The property must belong to the same client as the contract.
If something goes wrong
  • If the contract has no matching service, the target date cannot be calculated and the job cannot be saved.
  • Duplicate client work order references are flagged on import rather than creating two jobs.

FEA-18

Job Detail & Event Log

The full record of a job and everything that has happened to it.

All office usersClient users
How it works
  • Seven tabs: Overview, Other Details, SORs, Certificates, Media, Log and Financial.
  • The Log records every status change, allocation, resident contact, upload and approval, with who did it and when.
  • Overview shows the tasks beneath the job and their current state.
  • Notes can be added to the job at any point and are retained in the history.
Conditions
  • Requires View rights on the Jobs module.
  • Client users see only jobs belonging to their own organisation.
  • Record identifiers are obfuscated in the address bar, so records cannot be reached by guessing numbers.
If something goes wrong
  • The Log is append-only — entries cannot be edited or removed, so the history is always complete.

FEA-19

Job Rate Items & Valuation

The priced work attached to a job — what determines its cost and sell value.

All office users
How it works
  • Rate items are attached from the contract’s permitted catalogue, with variations applied where they exist.
  • Engineers can add further items on site for work actually carried out beyond what was planned.
  • Items are marked as chargeable or payable, separating what the client is billed from what the contractor is paid.
  • Totals for cost and sell roll up to the job and are shown on the Financial tab.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Jobs module; certain roles can view values but not change them.
  • Only rates attached to the job’s contract can be selected.
If something goes wrong
  • A job with no rate items attached has no sell value and cannot be cleared for invoicing.

FEA-20

Job Media & Documents

Photographs and documents attached to a job, from site or from the office.

All office users
How it works
  • Engineers upload photographs from the device during the visit; office users can attach documents at any point.
  • Files are stored in cloud storage and served back through the application so permissions still apply.
  • Media stays attached to the job permanently, forming part of the evidence trail.
Conditions
  • Uploads are validated on actual file content, not the file name.
  • Permitted types are restricted to images, documents and spreadsheets, within a maximum size.
If something goes wrong
  • A file of a disallowed type or over the size limit is rejected at upload with the reason shown.
Related: FEA-24, FEA-33  ·  Rules: BR-080 – BR-082
FEA-21

Bulk Job Communication

Messaging many residents at once about a batch of related work.

All office users
How it works
  • A set of jobs is selected and a single message issued to all their residents — typically to announce a planned visit window.
  • Messages use the same contract rules and client wording as automatic notifications, so tone stays consistent.
  • Individual jobs can also be messaged on their own.
  • Sends are queued and logged, and appear in the communications history.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Jobs module.
  • Residents must have a valid email address or mobile number recorded.
If something goes wrong
  • Recipients with no contact details are skipped and reported rather than failing the whole batch.
  • Messages that fail to deliver appear on the Failed SMS screen for follow-up.
Related: FEA-45, FEA-46  ·  Rules: BR-064
FEA-22

Task List & Allocation

The working list of site visits, and getting each one to an engineer.

Schedulers
How it works
  • One task represents one engineer visit; a job can have several.
  • Tasks are allocated to a named engineer, or placed in a work queue to be picked up.
  • Allocation state is shown as Unallocated, Assigned or In-Queue.
  • Tasks can be allocated in bulk, with an appointment availability check applied first.
  • The grid filters, sorts and pages on the server, with a slide-in detail panel per row.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Jobs module.
  • Engineers must be attached to the contract to appear in the allocation list.
  • Skills and service requirements narrow who can be allocated.
If something goes wrong
  • A bulk allocation where some tasks clash reports the clashes rather than double-booking.
Related: FEA-23, FEA-26, FEA-28  ·  Rules: BR-054, BR-055
FEA-23

Task Detail

What was found and done on a visit, with all its supporting evidence.

All office users
How it works
  • Five tabs: Log, SORs, Assets, Certificates and Media.
  • Status is drawn from an administrator-maintained list of 18 values, split between engineer outcomes and office decisions.
  • Appointment details, reschedule history and cancellation reasons are held on the task.
  • Engineer and resident signatures are stored, or the recorded reason why none was obtained.
Conditions
  • Requires rights on the Jobs module.
  • Editing a task an engineer has already started is restricted to senior roles.
If something goes wrong
  • A user without the elevated right sees the edit action unavailable on a started task rather than receiving an error on save.
Related: FEA-22, FEA-24, FEA-26  ·  Rules: BR-056, BR-057
FEA-24

On-Site Capture

What the engineer records during the visit, arriving live into the office record.

Engineers (mobile)
How it works
  • Progress is set through On Route, Arrived and a final outcome — each able to trigger a resident notification.
  • Rate items are added and commented on for work actually carried out.
  • Photographs and documents are uploaded against the task or a specific asset.
  • Assets can be added, linked or unlinked at the property.
  • Certificates are completed on the device and queued for office approval.
  • Where a value exceeds a defined threshold, the engineer must confirm it explicitly.
Conditions
  • Requires a role with mobile access.
  • Property warnings are shown before attendance.
  • Full behaviour, including offline working, is covered in the AJS Mobile Feature Documentation.
If something goes wrong
  • Where no signature can be obtained, a reason must be recorded instead — the task cannot be closed with neither.
Related: FEA-06, FEA-20, FEA-25, FEA-33  ·  Workflow: WF-06  ·  Rules: BR-058, BR-059
FEA-25

Signatures

Proof that the visit happened and the work was accepted.

Engineers (mobile)
How it works
  • Both an engineer signature and a resident signature can be captured on the device.
  • Signatures are stored against the task and appear on the printed job summary and certificates.
  • Where a resident is unavailable or declines, a no-signature reason is recorded instead.
Conditions
  • Requires a role with mobile access.

Key Workflows

Eleven end-to-end processes in detail, then seven more in brief.

WF-01

Onboarding a new client

Getting a new customer organisation ready to receive work.

  1. Create the client record with name, address and account details.
  2. Add contacts on both sides — the client’s people and the AJS staff managing the account.
  3. Load the property portfolio, individually or by spreadsheet upload.
  4. Set the finance terms — selling, billing, tax treatment and discounts.
  5. Attach the rate catalogue that will apply to this client’s work.
  6. Write the resident message wording for each event that will be notified.
  7. Raise the first contract. The client is now ready for work.
Decision — does this client exchange data with its own system?
WHENThe client runs its own housing management system
THENConfigure the Interface tab and complete code mapping before the first work order arrives
ELSEWork is raised manually or by the planned programme only

Steps 1–3 are the minimum to hold records. Steps 4–7 are required before work can be priced, communicated and delivered.

WF-02

Raising a reactive job

A repair is reported and needs to reach an engineer.

  1. Select the property and confirm the client and contract it falls under.
  2. Choose the service from the workstreams the contract permits.
  3. The system calculates the target date from the contract’s service terms.
  4. Set the priority, and flag as emergency if the situation requires it.
  5. Attach the priced rate items expected for the work.
  6. Record the client work order reference where one has been supplied.
  7. Save. One or more tasks are created beneath the job for the visits required.
  8. Allocate to an engineer, or place in a work queue.
Decision — does the resident need an appointment?
WHENThe work requires access to the property
THENThe task enters appointment booking and the resident is offered a slot (WF-05)
ELSEThe task is allocated directly and attended without a booked window

If the contract permits no matching service, the target date cannot be calculated and the job cannot be saved.

WF-03

Planned maintenance generation

Recurring compliance work is raised automatically, ahead of time.

  1. The master plan is defined on the contract — which work, on which properties, how often.
  2. A scheduled process runs each morning at 06:00 and checks what has fallen due.
  3. Jobs are created against the correct properties, priced and targeted from the contract.
  4. Tasks are generated beneath each job.
  5. Scheduling picks them up and appointments are offered to residents.
  6. The master plan list is updated to show what has been generated and what remains upcoming.
Decision — is the contract active?
WHENThe contract is active on the run date
THENPlanned work is generated as scheduled
ELSENo work is generated; the plan remains but produces nothing

Generation is once daily, not immediate on saving a plan. If a run does not complete, the work is picked up on the next run rather than skipped.

WF-04

Receiving a work order from a client system

Work raised in the client’s own system becomes a job in AJS without re-keying.

  1. A scheduled process collects waiting work orders, by direct connection or secure file transfer.
  2. The file lands in the Inbound folder and is picked up for processing.
  3. References are translated using the client’s code mapping — job type, priority, work category.
  4. The property is matched, normally by UPRN.
  5. A job is created, priced and targeted from the contract.
  6. The file moves to Processed and the counts on the integration dashboard update.
  7. As work progresses, updates and completions are returned to the client system automatically.
Decision — can every reference be resolved?
WHENThe property matches and every code maps to an AJS value
THENThe job is created and the file moves to Processed
ELSEThe record is held in the Errors folder for correction — it is never imported partially or incorrectly

Errored files are retained, not discarded, so they can be corrected and reprocessed. Failed transfers are retried on the next cycle.

WF-05

Booking and confirming an appointment

Agreeing a visit window with the resident, without a phone call where possible.

  1. The task appears on Pending Appointments awaiting a confirmed slot.
  2. Available windows are determined from the contract time slots, service duration and engineer availability.
  3. An offer is issued to the resident by email or text, carrying a secure single-purpose link.
  4. The resident opens the link and confirms the slot, or requests a different one.
  5. The task moves to a confirmed appointment and leaves the pending list.
  6. A reminder is issued ahead of the visit by an hourly scheduled process.
Decision — does the resident respond?
WHENThe resident confirms through the link
THENThe appointment is set and the reminder scheduled
ELSEThe task stays in a No Appointment state and remains on the pending list for the office to chase

Each client can define its own appointment workflow — the steps and rules that apply to booking for their properties.

WF-06

Delivering a task on site

The engineer’s visit, and how it reaches the office record.

  1. The task is released to the engineer and appears on their mobile task list.
  2. Property warnings are shown before attendance — safety and access notes for that address.
  3. The engineer sets On Route. The resident is notified and can track the approach.
  4. The engineer sets Arrived on reaching the property.
  5. Work is carried out — rate items added, readings taken, photographs captured, assets linked or added.
  6. Certificates are completed on the device where the work requires them.
  7. Signatures are captured from the engineer and the resident.
  8. The outcome is recorded and the visit closed.
Decision — can a resident signature be obtained?
WHENThe resident is present and willing to sign
THENThe signature is captured and stored against the task
ELSEA no-signature reason must be recorded — the visit cannot be closed with neither

Full field behaviour, including offline working and synchronisation, is covered in the AJS Mobile Feature Documentation.

WF-07

Handling a non-complete outcome

What happens when the visit could not finish the work.

  1. The engineer records the outcome — No Access, Further Works, Materials Required, H&S Fail, Asbestos Fail or Task Abandoned.
  2. A resident notification is issued where the contract has that event enabled.
  3. The office reviews the outcome and its supporting evidence.
  4. The appropriate follow-on is decided — rebook, raise new work, or escalate.
  5. The original task retains its outcome; nothing is overwritten.
Decision — what kind of outcome was it?
WHENNo Access — the resident was not home
THENThe task returns to scheduling to be rebooked
WHENFurther Works or Materials Required
THENA new job is raised for the additional work
WHENH&S Fail or Asbestos Fail
THENThe situation is escalated — these are safety outcomes, not scheduling ones

Every outcome is recorded in the job Log with who set it and when, so repeat No Access at an address is visible over time.

WF-08

Certificate production and approval

Turning a completed test into valid compliance evidence.

  1. An administrator builds the template from sections, fields and calculations.
  2. It is attached to the service or rate item that requires it.
  3. The engineer completes it on site, recording readings against the specific asset.
  4. On submission it is rendered to PDF and stored.
  5. It enters the Certificate Review queue in a Pending state.
  6. The reviewer decides — approve, reject, or update and approve.
  7. Approved certificates file automatically against the task, the job and the property.
Decision — does the certificate pass review?
WHENThe certificate is complete and correct
THENIt is approved, filed, and where configured transferred to the client system automatically
ELSEIt is rejected and returns to the engineer for correction; the original submission is retained alongside

Changing a template does not alter certificates already issued — those keep the form they were completed on.

WF-09

Clearing a job for invoicing

Establishing that delivered work is actually billable.

  1. The job completes — all its tasks done and approved.
  2. It appears on the Job Process List with its total costs and total sells side by side.
  3. Values are checked against the contract rates and any variations that apply.
  4. Discrepancies are corrected by adjusting the rate items attached to the job.
  5. The job is moved to Clear to Invoice, individually or in bulk.
  6. It becomes a candidate for an invoice batch.
Decision — does the job have a sell value?
WHENPriced rate items are attached and approved
THENThe job can be cleared for invoicing
ELSEThe job shows a dash rather than a value and cannot be cleared until items are attached

Large adjustments can be applied by spreadsheet round-trip — export the list, amend it, and re-import.

WF-10

Building an invoice batch

Grouping cleared jobs into one application or invoice.

  1. A batch code is reserved in sequence, so two users cannot claim the same one.
  2. Candidate jobs are listed — those cleared for invoicing on the same client and contract.
  3. Jobs are added to the batch.
  4. The batch total builds — net amount, VAT and total.
  5. Jobs can still be removed while the batch is being assembled.
  6. The batch status is moved on in one action once it is agreed.
Decision — is the batch ready to submit?
WHENAll intended jobs are included and values agreed
THENThe batch moves to APP Submitted and becomes visible in the invoice list
ELSEIt stays in APP Buildup and does not appear in the invoice list at all

A job removed from a batch returns to the candidate pool rather than being lost.

WF-11

Generating and issuing an invoice

Producing the document the client will pay against.

  1. The submitted batch is opened from the invoice list.
  2. Sell totals are calculated per job task.
  3. The invoice number is entered and checked as unique.
  4. The client reference is recorded so it matches their purchase records.
  5. The document is produced as a PDF or spreadsheet, according to the client’s requirement.
  6. It is stored against the batch with its date, reference and total.
  7. The jobs move to Invoiced.
Decision — is the invoice number already used?
WHENThe number is unused
THENThe document is produced and issued
ELSEThe number is rejected before anything is generated

An invoice can be raised as a credit note rather than a charge. Withdrawal requires a recorded reason and the record itself is retained, never erased.

Security & Access

What is implemented today. Nothing in this section is aspirational.

Sign-in and sessions

  • Signed session tokens issued on sign-in and checked on every request.
  • A separate renewal token extends sessions without re-entering a password.
  • Expired sessions renew silently and the original request is replayed.
  • Sign-out clears all stored session data from the browser.

Two-factor authentication

  • One-time passcode verification available as a second step after the password.
  • Codes are stored and validated per user against a dedicated record.
  • Built on a standard time-based one-time password mechanism.

Passwords

  • Stored using the platform’s current recommended one-way hashing algorithm.
  • An additional secret is combined with the password before hashing, so a stolen database alone cannot be tested against offline.
  • Never stored or transmitted in readable form.

Permissions

  • Eight rights per role per module — view, add, edit, delete, approve, import, export and client scope.
  • Menu items a role cannot view are removed before the page is drawn.
  • Rights are re-checked on the server for every request.
  • An extra restriction governs who may edit a task an engineer has already started.

Data handling

  • Record identifiers are obfuscated in page addresses, so records cannot be reached by guessing numbers.
  • Database access separates data from instructions, protecting against injected commands.
  • Optional encryption of request and response content, switchable per deployment.
  • Records are marked deleted rather than removed, preserving the audit trail.

Files and monitoring

  • Uploads validated on actual content, restricted by type and size.
  • Files stored in cloud storage and served back through the application.
  • A separate database holds application and interface logs, isolated from business data.
  • Failures in the user’s browser are reported back automatically with severity and context.
No credentials, keys or connection details appear anywhere in this document.

Configuration was reviewed in order to describe how the platform is secured; no secret value has been reproduced. All credentials live in environment configuration outside the code, held separately per environment.

Returning a rejected task to the engineer for correction.
RLO
Resident Liaison Officer — a resident-facing field role with its own mobile profile.
Rate Variation
A contract rate adjusted for particular units or addresses.
Remote Complete
An office decision state recording completion without a site visit.
SOR
Schedule of Rates — the priced catalogue of work items that makes a job costable.
Task
One engineer visit. A job may have several.
Task Status
Where a visit has reached. Eighteen exist, split between engineer outcomes and office decisions.
Threshold
A value limit above which an engineer must explicitly confirm what they have recorded.
Uplift
An agreed future increase to rates, applied on its effective date with advance reminders.
UPRN
Unique Property Reference Number — the national identifier used to match properties with client systems.
WIP
Work in Progress — a job whose costs are still accruing and which is not yet billable.
Work Order
The client’s own reference for a piece of work, carried on the job.
Workstream
A category of work a contract permits, containing the services within it.

Items observed in the live system that are intended but not confirmed working, or whose meaning could not be established. These must not be presented as working functionality.

NV-01An unlabelled commercial state
OBSERVEDA small number of jobs hold a financial status value that the screens have no label for and display as a dash.

NV-02Push channel for resident notifications

ACTIONConfirm whether it is live before including it in any client commitment.
NV-03Additional client API connections

ACTIONConfirm which are in live use before describing them as active integrations.
